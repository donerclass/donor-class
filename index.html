How to Use & FAQ

Donor Class Civic Intelligence • User Guide & Legal Verification
STEP 1

Select a Special Interest Sector

Click any sector pill above the map (Defense, Big Pharma, Wall Street, Energy, Big Tech, Foreign Policy, or Labor). The choropleth map dynamically recalculates color heat, rankings, and totals in real-time.

Tip: Toggle between sectors to see which regional delegations attract specific industry contributions.
STEP 2

Choose Your Ranking Metric

Use the metric controls to evaluate delegations fairly:

  • Total ($M): Cumulative PAC dollars injected into the state delegation.
  • Avg / Rep: Average PAC cash received per incumbent lawmaker.
  • % Funded: Percentage of state delegation receiving checks in that sector.
  • Per Capita: PAC spending per 100,000 citizens in the state.
STEP 3

Click a State or Search Lawmakers

Click a state on the interactive map or type a lawmaker's name in the search bar. This opens the Delegation Inspector drawer with:

  • Itemized PAC receipts across all 7 tracked sectors.
  • Direct candidate committee contributions vs. conduit PACs vs. independent Super PAC ad buys.
  • Bipartisan Democratic vs. Republican funding splits.
STEP 4

Share Custom Views & Export Data

Every state and sector selection updates your browser URL with shareable deep links (e.g. donorclass.info/?state=CA&ref=x). Copy and share exact views directly on social media, or click Export CSV in the header to download the raw dataset.

Where does this data come from?
All contribution records are calculated directly from official public disclosure filings submitted to the Federal Election Commission (FEC) for the 2024–2026 reporting cycles. We aggregate Schedule A (individual conduit/earmarked donations), Schedule B (direct multicandidate PAC disbursements), and 24/48-hour independent expenditure filings.
Are all 539 members of Congress tracked?
Yes. The tracker indexes all 100 U.S. Senators, 435 voting Representatives, and 4 non-voting territorial delegates across all 50 states and territories.
What is the difference between direct PAC money and Super PAC outside spending?
Under federal election law (52 U.S.C. § 30116(a)(2)(A)), contributions from multicandidate PACs directly to candidate committees are strictly capped at $5,000 per primary and $5,000 per general election ($10,000 per 2-year cycle). In contrast, independent expenditure Super PACs (such as United Democracy Project or Club for Growth) can raise unlimited funds to run non-coordinated independent broadcast and digital ads; under federal law, Super PACs cannot donate funds directly to candidate campaign committees.
Is Foreign Policy Advocacy foreign money?
No. Federal law (52 U.S.C. § 30121) strictly prohibits foreign nationals and foreign governments from contributing to U.S. elections. All tracked foreign policy advocacy PACs (such as AIPAC PAC, J Street, DMFI, RJC) are domestic American political committees registered with the FEC and funded exclusively by U.S. citizens.
Is DONOR CLASS partisan or funded by a political party?
100% nonpartisan and independent. The platform is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or aligned with any political party, candidate, or corporate PAC. The tracking formulas apply identically to Democrats, Republicans, and Independents alike.
Why does my state glow brighter or darker on the map?
The choropleth map dynamically shades states based on capital concentration in the active sector and metric. A brighter neon blue glow indicates higher funding totals, higher representative penetration, or higher per-capita exposure.
How do I look up my specific representative or senator?
Type your representative's last name (e.g. 'Jeffries', 'Schumer', 'McCaul', 'Khanna') into the search box in the header or directory. The table instantly filters and displays their itemized direct PAC, conduit, and outside spending totals.
Can I use this data for research, journalism, or reporting?
Yes. All data is public civic intelligence. You may freely cite, screenshot, link, or export CSV tables for investigative journalism, research papers, or community activism. Attribution to DONOR CLASS (donorclass.info) is appreciated.
Are there any tracking cookies, paywalls, or accounts required?
Zero paywalls, zero voter tracking cookies, and zero login requirements. The platform runs 100% client-side in your browser.

FEC Data Methodology & Sources

Donor Class is an independent, non-partisan civic technology platform built to illuminate how corporate and political action committees (PACs) fund both chambers of the U.S. Congress.

Tracked Sectors

  • Defense & Aerospace: Military contractors, defense electronics, aerospace weapons suppliers (Lockheed Martin, Raytheon, Boeing, General Dynamics).
  • Big Pharma & Health: Drug manufacturers, medical device firms, health insurance PACs (Pfizer, Eli Lilly, PhRMA, Blue Cross Blue Shield).
  • Wall St & Big Finance: Commercial banks, hedge funds, investment firms, credit union PACs (Goldman Sachs, American Bankers Assoc, Citadel, JPMorgan).
  • Fossil Fuels & Energy: Oil & gas supermajors, pipeline operators, utility energy PACs (Chevron, ExxonMobil, API, ConocoPhillips).
  • Big Tech & Telecom: Major digital platforms, broadband carriers, semiconductor PACs (Google/Alphabet, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft, AT&T).
  • Foreign Policy Advocacy: Domestic U.S. political committees and advocacy PACs focused on foreign policy, defense alliances, and pro-Israel diplomacy (AIPAC PAC, United Democracy Project, DMFI, J Street, RJC). All tracked PACs are domestic American entities funded by U.S. citizens; federal law (52 U.S.C. § 30121) strictly prohibits contributions from foreign nationals in U.S. elections.
  • Labor Unions: Organized trade unions, public sector worker unions (Teamsters, AFL-CIO, SEIU, IBEW, AFSCME).

Statutory Limits vs. Independent Outside Spending

Under federal election law (52 U.S.C. § 30116(a)(2)(A)), contributions from multicandidate PACs directly to candidate committees are strictly capped at $5,000 per election ($10,000 per 2-year cycle). Independent expenditures by Super PACs (such as United Democracy Project) are non-coordinated outside broadcast and digital ad buys; under federal law, Super PACs cannot donate funds to candidate campaign committees. Earmarked contributions bundled through conduit PACs (such as AIPAC PAC or ActBlue/WinRed under 11 CFR § 110.6) represent aggregated individual donations forwarded directly to candidates.
